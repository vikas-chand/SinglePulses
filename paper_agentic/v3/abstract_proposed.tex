\begin{abstract}
Time-resolved spectroscopy of gamma-ray bursts (GRBs) rests on judgement
calls. Which detectors to trust, where the background lies, where the
burst begins and ends, how finely to bin it in time, and which of two
dozen spectral models to adopt are all decided by hand. We describe the
design of an agent that makes those calls for a campaign of 106
single-pulse \textit{Fermi}/GBM bursts. A human approves every step. The
language model runs inside a harness: deterministic tools, plain-language
skill documents, state derived from disk, and enforced boundaries.
Specialist roles read, produce, and verify, and a producer never verifies
its own work. Each burst is analysed blind. Its numbers are frozen before
any comparison with the published literature, and every mismatch is
attributed and distilled into a numbered lesson with an executable test.
We present the harness component by component, the guides and sensors
that act at each step, and the object-and-action model that binds every
product to its inputs by hash. On the walkthrough burst the design caught
engine defects, which were fixed and tested, and confirmed blind
predictions while refuting one. The trained artifact is the skill library,
readable by a student or by another AI system. We state plainly where the
human gate still decides, and we release the library, the engine, the
tests, and the per-burst records.
\end{abstract}
