### 1a. There is no "GRB 150630A" in the record — do not create one
`GCNIntelligence.md` forbids assuming the `A` suffix. Here the evidence is unusually clean:

- **von Kienlin+2020 (4th GBM catalog) Table 4 leaves the `GRB` column BLANK** for
  `bn150630223`. That column is defined as *"Gamma-ray burst identifier (YYMMDDA)"* and it

- **Zero GCN circulars** exist under any 150630 form (see `bn150630223_gcn_raw.txt`), so no

**⚠ T1-adjacent trap for downstream users.** *Maccary+2025* prints **`GRB150630A`** for our
trigger and **`GRB150630B`** for `bn150630958` (VizieR J/A+A/702/A95 table1, recno 1449/1450).
That is **their internal same-day-ordering convention**, not a literature name: 05:20:47 UT
gets `A`, 22:59:00 UT gets `B`. Their `n_GRB` flag (`*` = *"GRB not appear with a GCN-style

## 3. INSTRUMENT STORY

**Detected by four instruments; analysed by one.**

von Kienlin+2020 Table 4 `Other` column = **`K, IA, Mo`**, decoded from the catalog's own
Note (3): `K` = Konus-Wind, `IA` = INTEGRAL SPI-ACS, `Mo` = Mars Observer/Odyssey. So beyond
GBM this burst was seen by three more instruments spanning an interplanetary baseline — the
raw material for an IPN triangulation.

**But no IPN box or Konus spectrum was ever published for it.** Checked and came up empty:
- IPN supplements: Hurley+2013 (BATSE era) and Hurley+2017 (supplement to the *2nd* GBM
  catalog, coverage ends 2012) — neither reaches 2015. ADS `title:"Interplanetary Network
  Supplement" year:2018-2026` → **numFound 0**: no supplement to the 3rd or 4th GBM catalog
  exists.
- Konus-Wind: the published KW catalogs are redshift-selected (Tsvetkova+2017, 2021 — we have
  no z) or short-GRB-selected (Svinkin+2016; Lysenko+2025 — our T90 = 22.8 s is long).
  The ioffe.ru per-burst pages 404 for `GRB150630_T19245/6/7` and the index returns HTTP 403.

**Fermi-LAT: not in the field of view.** Boresight angle computed here from the poshist
quaternions = **112.3° at T0** (112.4° at SRC_START, 110.6° at SRC_STOP) — far outside the
LAT FoV, with no slew. Consistent with `Other` omitting `L`, and with `HAS_LAT = False` in
`results/grb_sample.ecsv`. **The absence of `gll_*`/LAT products in `data/bn150630223/` is
therefore CORRECT, not a fetch gap** — a distinction that matters under the
take-data-in-every-band doctrine (contrast bn110928180, where the same absence *was* a gap).

**GBM detector story.** Only two NaIs ever see this burst well: n4 at 23.05° and n5 at 56.45°
(next nearest is n3 at 67.19°). The catalog's own choices match ours exactly —
`bcat_detector_mask` = n4+n5 (durations), `scat_detector_mask` = n4+n5+b0 (spectra); our
approved set is **n4, n5, b0**.

## 5. ⚠ FRAME WARNING CARRIED INTO STEPS 5–7: the burst sits on the Earth's limb

Computed here from `glg_poshist_all_150630_v01.fit` at the GBM catalog position, verified
with two independent primitives (astropy `SkyCoord.separation` and the project's own
`_angular_distance`; spacecraft latitude reproduces poshist `SC_LAT` exactly). Full table in
`results/qc/bn150630223_step1_response_coverage.ecsv`.

The source **crossed the Earth's geometric limb at T0 − 5.2 s** and climbs away from it at
0.063 °/s. Line-of-sight tangent height through the atmosphere:

| epoch | geocentre angle | elevation above limb | tangent height |
|---|---|---|---|
| BKG_NEG start (−91 s) | 61.73° | **−5.42°** | source **occulted** |
| BKG_NEG stop (−11 s) | 66.75° | **−0.41°** | source **occulted** |
| SRC_START (−2 s) | 67.31° | +0.16° | **7 km** |
| T0 | 67.44° | +0.28° | **13 km** |
| +5 s | 67.75° | +0.60° | 28 km |
| +11.5 s | 68.19° | +1.03° | 47 km |
| SRC_STOP (+23 s) | 68.88° | +1.72° | 78 km |
| BKG_POS start (+45 s) | 70.26° | +3.10° | 136 km |

Two consequences, both recorded as hazards rather than conclusions:

1. **The entire pre-burst background window is measured with the source below the limb.**
   Background windows measure background, so this is not wrong — but the pre- and post-burst
   windows sample *different* Earth-albedo geometries (tangent height −282 km → +314 km
   across them), so the polynomial is interpolating across a real geometric gradient.
2. **The atmospheric column in front of the source falls monotonically across exactly our
   source window.** A systematic that co-evolves with the light curve is the most dangerous
   kind: it can mimic both a FRED-like rise and a hard-to-soft evolution.

**The tension that must not be hidden:** the GBM team did **not** flag this burst for partial
Earth occultation, and GBM plainly recorded a bright 24 ph/cm²/s pulse — which a 13 km
tangent height could not transmit. The most likely resolution is that the **true position is
several degrees further from the limb than the catalog position** (GBM systematic
localisation error is ~3–4°, and the quoted 1.46° is statistical only). That is itself a
useful, falsifiable inference: *the burst's detection is independent evidence that the
catalog position is biased toward the Earth.* Alternative resolutions (a different
occultation criterion in the catalog pipeline; an error in the poshist-based reconstruction
that two independent primitives failed to expose) are not excluded. **Do not resolve this in
the fit — carry it as a named alternative hypothesis with the falsification tests written
into P0 (P-E, P-F).**
