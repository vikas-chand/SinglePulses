### 3.1 Light-curve morphology — and why it defines the component-coverage labels
- GCN 13785: "The GBM light curve shows a **single bright pulse** with a duration (T90) of about
  22.5 s (50–300 keV)."
- GCN 13787 (Konus): "The light curve shows a **single pulse with a duration of ~8 s followed by
  a weaker and softer emission till ~T0+30 s**. The emission in the main bursting episode is seen
  up to **7 MeV**."

So the event is **MAIN PULSE (≈ T0 → T0+8 s) + SOFT TAIL (≈ 8 → 30 s)**. Those two labels are
used as the T9 *component coverage* tag on every published number in §4.

Cataloged, **not** analysis targets for this prompt-emission module.

- **Afterglow (`module-future; afterglow`).** The counterpart search **failed at every wavelength**:

  **neither fades** over 134 → 573 ks (GCN 13821), explicitly concluding "we have not detected any

## 8. What the literature *wants* our prompt spectrum to be (disclosure for P3)

Two pressures, pulling opposite ways, both to be disclosed rather than obeyed:

1. **Toward synchrotron, away from a photosphere.** Acuner+2020 fit *physical* models to this
   burst's brightest bin and found the non-dissipative photosphere disfavoured by
   Δln Z = 40.3 against slow-cooled synchrotron (P = 0.98). Ryde+2019 and Acuner+2020 both put
   α ≈ −0.65 at peak flux — comfortably *below* the −2/3 slow-cooling line. A reader primed by
   these papers expects **no significant blackbody**.
2. **Toward "some bins are above the line of death" — or not.** Wang+2024 records `α > −2/3` as
   **"not all"** (some bins are above); Li+2026 records `f_{−2/3} = **0.00**` (none are). These
   are **incompatible statements about the same burst** (see §9).

Nobody has ever fitted a blackbody, a two-break, or any additive component to this burst in the
refereed literature. **Whatever our engine returns for BB and DSBPL here is genuinely new**, and
correspondingly has no external check — which is the argument for taking the P0 predictions in
`bn120919309_P0_frozen.json` seriously as the only pre-registration available.

## 9. Recorded internal inconsistencies (T5 / L26 — logged, NOT resolved)

1. **α vs the line of death.** Wang+2024 "not all" (⇒ some bins with α > −2/3) vs Li+2026
   `f_{−2/3} = 0.00` (⇒ none). Likely cause: Wang fits Band, Li fits CPL, and CPL indices run
   systematically softer when β is steep — but **that is a hypothesis, not a resolution.**
2. **Where the Ep maximum sits.** Li+2026 puts the Ep peak **1.16 ± 0.86 s before** the flux peak
   (t_p(Ep) = 2.27 vs t_p(F) = 3.43 s). Lu+2018's Ep maximum is the 2.34–3.09 s bin and its KRL
   flux peak is t_m = 3.21 ± 0.06 s — the same ordering, but their Ep bin *contains* the flux
   peak. Yu+2019's CPL Ep maximum among constrained bins is 1.87–2.68 s, i.e. *before* 3.072–4.096 s
   (the catalog peak-flux interval). Direction agrees; magnitude does not.
3. **β.** GBM catalog `flnc_band` β = −2.089 ± 0.05 over −1.024→28.672 s; GCN 13785 β = −2.15 ± 0.06
   over 0.003→10.496 s; Yu+2019's per-bin Band β runs −2.3 … −4.0. A β near −4 is not physically
   distinguishable from "no high-energy tail measured", and Yu's own ΔDIC says Band beats CPL by
   only 1–9 in those bins. **Do not treat published β for this burst as a measurement without
   checking its error bar.**
4. **Lu+2018's last bin** (7.26–10.56 s) reports `α = −0.53 ± 0.00` — a **zero error bar**, i.e.
   a railed/failed uncertainty. Flagged, not used.
5. **Lu+2018's `d` (KRL decay index) = 25.86 ± 22.45** — an 87 % relative error. The pulse's
   decay index is effectively unconstrained in that fit, which sits awkwardly beside Li+2026's
   well-defined `t_d = 1.31 s`. Different pulse models, not necessarily a contradiction; recorded.
6. **A citation that does not resolve.** Wang F.+2020 (`2020ApJ...893...77W`) cites GCN 13787
   (the Konus circular for *this* burst) in its bibliography, yet the string `120919` appears
   **nowhere** in its 210 pages (checked twice, via text extraction and via `search_for`).
   **Recorded as unresolved; this burst is NOT claimed as a member of that sample.**
