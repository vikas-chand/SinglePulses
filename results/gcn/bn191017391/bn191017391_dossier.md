## Instrument story

1. **09:23:39 UT** — GBM triggers (592997024). The flight software **misclassifies the
   trigger as a particle event**, so no automatic GRB circulars/notices follow.
2. **09:51 UT** — the MPE BALROG pipeline, which fits public trigdat regardless of
   classification, issues the first circular: GCN 26016 (position RA 169.0, Dec +44.7).
3. **14:22 UT** — the GBM team corrects the record, GCN 26020 verbatim: *"was
   misclassified by the GBM flight software as a particle event but is in fact due to a
   GRB. Therefore, the Fermi GBM team reports the detection of GRB 191017A."*
4. Nothing else follows — the ~1.5° GBM-only localization plus the 131° LAT boresight
   angle meant no narrow-field follow-up; **2 circulars total**, no redshift, no afterglow.
   The mundane consequence of the misclassification: this otherwise bright burst
   (fluence 2.05e-5 erg/cm², top-quartile) has **zero refereed papers** (see harvest).
