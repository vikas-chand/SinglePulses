## Instrument story

Fermi-GBM triggered at 08:47:36.383 UT on the 2048-ms, 47–291 keV algorithm. GBM never
issued a circular of its own (`tcat GCN_FLAG = No`); the burst entered the record through the
IPN. Seven instruments detected it: **Fermi-GBM, Konus-Wind, INTEGRAL SPI-ACS, MESSENGER NS,
Mars Odyssey (HEND + GRS), Swift-BAT (outside the coded FOV, flag `d` in Hurley+2017 = *"Burst
was outside the coded field of view of the BAT, and not localized by it"*), and AGILE**
(the AGILE detection appears only in Hurley+2017 Table 2 — `AGI,INT,Kon,MES,MO,Swi` — and in
von Kienlin+2014's "other detections" column as `A`; GCN 12077 does not mention AGILE).
Triangulation shrank the localization from **579 deg² (3σ GBM) to 1.53 deg² (3σ IPN)**, a
factor **378** (Hurley+2017 Table 6). No LAT/LLE detection is claimed anywhere, and none is
expected: at θ_SC = 145° the source was 55° *behind* the LAT.

The GBM light curve (quick-look `glg_lc_bcat_bn110618366_v00.png`, n2+na, three bands) is a
**single FRED-like pulse**: gentle rise from ≈ −20 s, peak at ≈ +18–25 s, long quasi-exponential
decay merging into background by ≈ +100–120 s in 50–300 keV. There is no second pulse and no
precursor.

### E. Golkhou, Butler & Littlejohns 2015 — *The Energy-Dependence of GRB Minimum Variability Timescales* (2015ApJ...811...93G)
Table 2 ("GRB Minimum Timescales") row, p. 24 of the PDF; column alignment confirmed by word

`110618366 | 110618A | Δt_min = 12.874 ± 0.363 s | Δt_S/N = 1.755 ± 0.152 s | T90 = 163.843 ± 11.406 s | T_start,100 = −59.600 s | T_stop,100 = +241.866 s | σ_X,tmin = 0.54 | σ_X,tS/N = 0.07 | z = …`
- **Definition + band matter (T3):** `Δt_min` is the timescale at which their **Haar-wavelet
  scaleogram** σ_X(Δt) departs from the linear uncorrelated-noise rise (broken-power-law fit) —
  **not** a pulse width and **not** our MVT estimator; `Δt_S/N` is the Poisson noise floor, the
  shortest *measurable* timescale. Band = **full 8 keV–1 MeV**, 200 µs binning (their §2).
  Diff only against a like-for-like estimator in the same band.
- **Their T90 column is not independent:** §2 states the T90 estimates come from *"Table 7 of
  von Kienlin et al. (2014)"* — which is why it matches to 3 decimals. Do not double-count it.
- **Δt_min = 12.9 s is enormous** — among the smoothest bursts in their sample, quantitatively
  confirming the Konus "unusually gentle onset" and the single-FRED morphology. Note
  `Δt_S/N = 1.755 ± 0.152` is *numerically identical* to the neighbouring row (110613631);
  recorded as-is, flagged as a possible quantization/duplication artifact — verify at source
  before using it in a correlation.

## BEYOND-PROMPT CONTEXT (future modules)
- **Afterglow (`module-future;afterglow`)** — the entire afterglow record is GCN 12081: one
  SMARTS/CTIO I-band epoch at +113 h covering 6 % of the IPN box, no new source vs DSS. There
  is no X-ray, radio, or host observation. For a future afterglow module this burst is a
  **negative control**: extremely intense prompt emission (KW fluence 1.1×10⁻⁴ erg/cm², top
  percentile) with *zero* counterpart information, purely because the localization was
  IPN-only and 1.5 deg² wide. It quantifies what the pre-Swift-style localization regime costs.
- **Cosmology (`module-future;cosmo`)** — no z ⇒ this burst can only ever sit in the
  *no-redshift control sample* of Ep–Eiso / Amati-plane studies (the Heussaff+2013-style
  selection-effect arm). Its very high fluence + moderate observed Ep make it a useful
  selection-effect data point precisely because it is unmeasurable.
- **No SN, no multimessenger, no polarization literature exists for this burst.**
- **Bias disclosure for P3:** because nothing was ever published about this burst's spectrum
  beyond two catalog rows and one preliminary GCN, there is **no literature prior pushing us
  toward any component** — no photospheric claim, no synchrotron claim, no two-break claim.
  That makes it a comparatively clean unpublished-decision test.
