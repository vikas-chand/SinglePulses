## ⏱ The Konus 3.137 s T0 offset — PLAUSIBLY RESOLVED (was "unresolved" in P3/§12)
GBM T0 = 04:47:43.75; Konus T0 = 04:47:46.887 (GCN 12191) → +3.137 s in UT. The reconciliation
record left the mapping ambiguous. The circulars settle it empirically:
- GBM (12187): *"The 1-sec peak photon flux measured starting from **T0+1.98 s**"* (10–1000 keV);
- Konus (12191): *"a 64-ms peak flux, measured from **T0+1.984 s**"* (20 keV–10 MeV).

Two instruments put the burst's peak at the **same offset from their own T0** to 4 ms. If the
Konus timebase were 3.137 s later *in burst phase*, they would disagree about the peak epoch by
3.1 s. The parsimonious reading is that **the 3.137 s is a propagation/timebase difference, not a
phase offset** — Wind sits near L1 (~5 light-seconds from Earth), and IPN triangulation (GCN
12195) is built from exactly these inter-spacecraft delays. ⇒ **Adopt the same-T0 phase mapping**
(the reading P2 already provisionally assumed), now with evidence.
⚠ Status **PLAUSIBLE, not proven**: the two quoted numbers are different measurements (1-s photon
flux vs 64-ms energy flux, different bands). **Falsification path:** project the Wind–Fermi
baseline onto the IPN direction (332.456, −38.628) and check it predicts ≈3.14 s.
