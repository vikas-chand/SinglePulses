## 3. Instrument story

GBM triggered first (04:04:25.06); Swift/BAT triggered 5.15 s later and slewed immediately,
XRT on target at T_BAT+53 s and UVOT at +62 s. Konus-Wind triggered 9.8 s after GBM and saw
emission **up to ~7 MeV**. INTEGRAL SPI-ACS detected it independently. **Fermi-LAT had the
burst 31.4° off boresight — i.e. inside the field of view — yet no LAT/LLE circular was
issued and no LAT/LLE detection is reported anywhere in the harvested literature.** Swift
began entering the SAA at T_BAT+~200 s and BAT event collection stopped at T_BAT+395 s.

Optically this is one of the most-studied GRB afterglows ever: extremely red (J−K ≈ 2.4 at
10 min), and it became the textbook case for **time-resolved dust destruction** (Morgan+2014).
Radio (EVLA 5.8 GHz) and submm (SMA 225 GHz) were both non-detections.

**Detectors in our stamped selection: n9 (43.66°), na (50.58°), nb (18.39°), b1 (16.16°)** —
approved by Vikas Chand 2026-07-19T22:18:36Z, `human_gui`, `accepted_suggestion`, source
window **[−1.0, +47.0] s**, backgrounds [−32, −10] and [+55, +145] s.
This is **exactly** the GBM catalog's `scat_detector_mask = 00000000011101` → n9, na, nb, b1.
Independent corroboration of the Stage-1 detector choice.

(Konus quote E_p,rest = 265 ± 35 keV from *their* 153 keV and z = 1.73 — that is the

## 5. BEYOND-PROMPT CONTEXT (future modules)

Scope note (`LiteratureHarvest.md` Phase 3c): our ANALYSIS is prompt-only; the reading below
is cataloged for the future sibling modules and carries `module-future` tags in
`bn120119170_harvest.json`. **GRB 120119A's literature footprint is overwhelmingly
afterglow/dust — 35 of the 43 refereed hits.**

**Dust destruction (the burst's signature result).** Morgan+2014 (MNRAS 440, 1810), *"Evidence
for dust destruction from the early-time colour change of GRB 120119A"*, is the reason this
burst is famous: PAIRITEL + GROND caught a colour change in the first minutes and it is read
as dust being destroyed by the burst's own UV/X-ray flux. Japelj+2014 Table (p. 25) records
120119A with z = 1.728, A_V = 1.09 ± 0.16, extinction law **FM** (not SMC — unusual), host
column < 21. This single object then anchors a decade of extinction-curve work: Zafar+2016,
Wiseman+2017, Corre+2018, Zafar+2018, Heintz+2018/2019a/b/c, Japelj+2015, Hoang+2020,
Rakotondrainibe+2024, Cappellazzo+2022.

**Afterglow physics.** Gompertz+2018 (*The Environments of the Most Energetic GRBs*) finds
120119A in an **ISM** (not wind) environment, ν_R < ν_x < ν_c, ε_B ≈ 0.01, and — relevantly for
method-quality — flags it as one of two bursts where *"the UKSSDC automatic fitting routine
gives obviously erroneous results"* and where their five independent estimators of the
electron index p disagree (p = 2.28 ± 0.08 to 3.32 ± 0.09). XRT: initial decay α = 2.58 ± 0.08
breaking at T+167 s to α = 1.008 (+0.017/−0.018); WT Γ = 1.93 ± 0.07, N_H,host excess
2.18e21 cm⁻² (GCN 12877). Japelj+2014 includes it in a reverse-shock phenomenology sample.

**Host galaxy / ISM.** Selsing+2019 (X-shooter GRB legacy sample); Heintz+2019 (neutral carbon
survey), Heintz+2019 (H₂-bearing DLA), Hashimoto+2019 (ALMA [CII] deficit, SFR of the
z ≈ 2 host), Metha+2021 and Yates+2021 (chemical-inhomogeneity / metallicity modelling),
Wang+2018 (host offsets), Corre+2018.

**Polarimetry.** Steele+2017 (RINGO2 sample) — a future polarization module inherits this.

**Cosmology / LIV / multimessenger.** Shao+2017 and Jiang+2021 supply the lag data;
Wei+2022 (anisotropic LIV from lag transitions) and Amelino-Camelia+2026 (GRB neutrinos with
quantum-spacetime redshift leverage) use 120119A downstream. Ahlgren+2020 tested it for
gravitational lensing (no lensed pair). Dainotti+2024 (optical GRB catalogue with z),
Kann+2024a/b, Llamas Lanza+2024 (SVOM/ECLAIRs detection biases), Becerra+2023,
Yamazaki+2020, Wang+2020 place it in population samples.

**What the literature *wants* the prompt spectrum to be (the P3 bias disclosure).** Jiang+2021
is the only paper that puts a prompt-emission physical model on this burst, and it is a
**photospheric** one: they use the lag to place the emission radius at
r_ph(E₂) = 8.47 (+20.21/−0.77) au with 1/k_r = 0.100 (+0.438/−0.078), and note that
*"for GRB 120119170, the large uncertainty of 1/k_r means that r_ph(E₂) can be as large as
24.2 au in the extreme case"* and that the implied B₁ range *"has no physical sense"* at the
upper end. So there is a weak photospheric prior in the literature, resting entirely on a
lag measurement with a 5× uncertainty. Nothing in the record claims a blackbody component,
and nothing claims a low-energy break — **the two-break question is untouched for this
burst.** Our fits are effectively unconstrained by prior expectation, which is the good case.

## 6. Open items handed to later steps

1. **LAT/LLE data are missing and should not be** (`feedback_take_data_every_band`, L17).
   GCN 12874 puts the burst at **31.4° from the LAT boresight** — inside the FoV — yet
   `data/bn120119170/` holds GBM only (no `gll_*`, no `LAT/`). Inclusion is gated on data
   quality, never on detection significance, so the LLE/LAT arm should be acquired and
   quality-tested before this burst is called GBM-only.
2. **Window vs T90.** Our source stop (+47 s) precedes the catalog T90 end (+58.4 s) and the
   catalog fit interval end (+57.3 s). Every published time-integrated number is on a longer
   interval than ours. Not necessarily wrong — but it must be stated in the P3 diff and it
   biases us against the soft late emission.
3. **`na` sits at 50.58°**, just outside the project's ≤50° NaI preference; it is inside the
   human-approved selection and inside the GBM catalog's own detector mask, so it stands —
   flagged for the record, not challenged.
4. **Background window −32.0 s starts 0.2 s after the TTE file begins (−32.2 s).** Passes,
   but with no margin; any re-selection that moves the pre-burst window earlier will run off
   the end of the data.
5. **Position provenance** — use the XRT enhanced position (120.02875, −9.08177), not the
   `fermigbrst` Dec.
