## 3. Redshift — verbatim

> *"The spectrum reveals strong absorption lines of MgII2796, MgII2803, MgI2853, FeII2586,
> FeII2600, and Ca H&K. **A provisional reduction gives a redshift of z=0.597, which we believe
> is most likely the redshift of this burst.**"*
> — Cucchiara & Fumagalli, **GCN 14207**, Lick Shane 3-m / KAST, 2×600 s, 3500–8000 Å,
> ~50 min after the BAT trigger.

- **Method:** metal **absorption** lines in the afterglow spectrum. **Confidence wording:**
  "provisional reduction", "most likely" — the circular hedges; it is not a published,
  refereed measurement.
- **Independent corroboration (different method):** GCN 14303 (de Ugarte Postigo et al.,
  GTC/OSIRIS, +25.8 d): SNID template fit *"By leaving the redshift unconstrained we obtain the
  same template fit and derive a redshift of **z = 0.58 ± 0.02**, fully consistent with the
  absorption line redshift."*
- **Caveat recorded, not resolved (T5):** Modjaz+2016 §2 treats it as a **lower limit** —
  *"since the redshift of this SN/GRB is uncertain and there is only a lower limit of z=0.597"*
  (2016ApJ...832..108M p.5). Everyone else (Cano+2014, Kovacevic+2014, Li 2023, Poolakkil+2021,
  Zhu+2024, Wang+2024, Amelino-Camelia+2026) uses z = 0.597 as a measurement.
- **Prompt-module relevance:** z enters no Stage-1/5/6 decision. It matters only for the
  rest-frame quantities in §5 — where **half the published frames are (1+z)-scaled and half are
  not** (see the L21 warnings inline).

## 4. Instrument story (what actually happened)

1. **GBM triggered first** (01:31:26.024 UT) on a 1024 ms timescale in 47–291 keV, then Swift/BAT
   4 s later (01:31:30). The GBM *notice* reached the ground ~600 s before the BAT one because
   the BAT trigger fell inside a TDRSS downlink: GCN 14214 verbatim — *"the BAT trigger occurred
   during a telemetry downlink session … For this trigger the minimum bufferring delay to the
   ground was 692 sec."* ROTSE therefore tiled the **GBM** error box first and missed the OT
   (GBM position was 3.78° off; GCN 14208).
2. **Swift could not slew** (Moon constraint until 2013-02-18 14:00 UT) ⇒ **no XRT, no UVOT**
   at all. This is why the burst has *no X-ray afterglow* (Littlejohns+2015 drop it from their
   sample for exactly this reason; Lü+2018 footnote 2 likewise).
3. **Fermi did not slew either** — θ_LAT = 86°, far off-axis, no LAT/LLE data.
4. Bright optical/NIR afterglow, mm detection at 93 GHz, SN 2013ez spectroscopically confirmed.

**Data-quality note carried into Step 1:** the local TTE files trigger 3ML's
*"contains duplicate time tags and is thus invalid. Contact the FSSC"* warning for n1, n2 and b0
(seen in `results/clean_per_burst_human_final/bn130215063/refit.log`). Recorded, not acted on.

**MULTI-INSTRUMENT CONSENSUS (Rossi+2026 §4.1 procedure, per LiteratureHarvest Phase 4).**
Do **not** pick a favourite Ep here. The honest statement of the prompt spectral shape is:

> Across BAT (15–150 keV, Γ = 1.59 ± 0.14), WAM (Γ = 1.61 +0.08/−0.07) and GBM
> (`flnc_plaw` index −1.457 ± 0.012), three independent instruments over three bands all measure
> a **power law of photon index ≈ 1.6 with no required curvature**. Every quoted "Ep" is
> therefore a model artefact of where each fitter chose to place a break in a nearly featureless
> spectrum: 70, 102, 155, 207, 210, 248, 335, 396, 411, 1480, 2387, 2439, 5390 keV are all the
> *same data*. **Any Amati/Yonetoku-plane placement of this burst must carry an
> inter-instrument-and-inter-model systematic that dominates the statistical error.**

### 🔴 Traps hit on this burst — recorded, not resolved

- **T6 (chained citations rot).** **Ep = 155 ± 63 keV** originates in GCN 14219, which declares
  itself *preliminary*. It is then quoted as the burst's Ep by **Cano+2014 §3**
  ("Ep = 155 ± 63 keV (Younes & Bhat 2013)") → **Cano+2017 review** Table (`130215A 2013ez GRB
  0.597 65.7 3.1(1.6) **155(63)** 7.53e50`) → **Lü+2018** Table 2 (`130215A/2013ez Swift/Fermi
  0.597 66 **155±63** 10.16↑`) → **Fana Dirirsa+2019** (Ep,rest = 247.54 ± 100.61 = 155×1.597).
  The GBM catalog superseded it in 2014 with `flnc_band` Ep = **209.95 ± 42.31 keV** over a
  *different* interval. **Verify at the primary source before any diff uses 155 keV.**
- **T7 (claims are continuum-relative).** "Best model" for this burst is *entirely* a statement
  about which continuum was offered: catalog says **SBPL** (of 4), Li 2023 says **Band** (of 2,
  by ΔAIC = −1.7 — i.e. indistinguishable), Chang+2026 says **Band** (of 6, by BIC), our archival
  says **CPL+BB** by AIC and **CPL** by BIC (of 6). Record the menu with the verdict.
- **T8 (published statistic conventions).** Li 2023's `likeli/AIC/BIC` triple is internally
  consistent (COMP 3523/7053/7065 with k = 3 ⇒ AIC = 2·3523 + 2·3 = 7052 ≈ 7053; BIC adds
  ln N ⇒ +12), so **their "likeli" column stores a positive −lnL-like statistic, not lnL** —
  exactly the Wang+2019 pattern. Verified by re-deriving one row before use.
  Separately, **Poolakkil+2021 Table 5's COMP/BAND `E_peak^rest` columns look swapped or
  differently-derived for this burst** (COMP 411 keV, BAND 5390 keV, whereas the HEASARC

- **T5 (papers contradict).** Li 2023 Table 2's **Band** `E_p^rest` column repeats the *observed*

## 6. BEYOND-PROMPT CONTEXT (future modules) — per Phase 3c

Cataloged, summarised, **not** analysis targets for this module.

**Afterglow (`module-future; afterglow`).**
Optical/NIR only — no X-ray at all (Moon constraint). Single power-law decay α = 1.25 ± 0.11 up
to 1 d, then **a plateau to ~6 d**, then a break to α₂ = 3.28 ± 0.25 at T_B = 6.39 ± 0.35 d
(Cano+2014 §3.2, §3.5). Cano+2014's headline: the optical/NIR bolometric LC is fit by the
Zhang & Mészáros (2001) **millisecond magnetar** energy-injection model with **P₀ = 12.0 ms,
B = 1.1 × 10¹⁵ G, L_plat = 6.1 × 10⁴⁴ erg/s, T_plat = 2.3 × 10⁵ s (rest-frame)**. Independent
mm detection at 93 GHz, 2.99 ± 0.39 mJy (GCN 14210). RATIR SED (Littlejohns+2015): LMC-law
preferred, A_V = 0.19 (+0.03/−0.04) mag; r/i/Z/Y/J at 11 h = 17.47/17.22/16.94/16.80/16.72.
Li+2018 afterglow catalog decomposes it as Global 0.7–1517.9 s / Normal-Decay 0.7–500 s (PL) /
SN-Bump 499.96–1517.9 s (BKPL), colour index 0.90 ± 0.20. Becerra+2023 (TAROT/COATLI/RATIR)
include it in their optical-rise statistics.

**Supernova (`module-future; sn`).**
**SN 2013ez.** Cano+2014 measure blueshifted Fe II λ5169 and Si II λ6355 giving an
**unusually low expansion velocity ~4000–6350 km/s — "the lowest ever measured for a GRB-SN"** —
and classify it **Ic, not Ic-BL**. Modjaz+2016 dispute the classification (*"SNID finds good
matches with both SNe Ic-bl … and with SNe Ic. Thus, we call it a 'SN Ic/Ic-bl'"*) and exclude it
from their mean Ic-bl spectra. Klose+2019: *"For GRB 130215A, the SN is only detected at low
significance, we cut the light curve off at 17 days; no host is detected."* Host galaxy
undetected to g > 26.2, r > 26.1, i > 25.1, J > 23.2 ⇒ M_g > −16.5 (Cano+2014 §3.4).
Graham+2016 use it as the example of *"LGRBs arising from non-broad-line SNe events"*.
Lü+2018 SN properties: M_peak = −18.85 ± 0.15, 6.41 ± 0.34, 0.375 ± 0.025.

**Progenitor classification (`module-future; classification`, but prompt-fed).**
Nuessle+2024 SVM on `Ep,prompt·S_γ⁻¹` vs T90: `GRB 130215063 | 143.746 | 4.06E+06 |
P_collapsar = 0.530 | P_merger = 0.470` — i.e. **the classifier is essentially undecided on a
burst with a spectroscopic SN**, which they use as a training/known-progenitor case. Strain+2026
and Chen+2025 (t-SNE) and Zhu+2024 (ML) all carry it as a labelled massive-star/SN GRB.
Jespersen+2020 list it among the 11 Swift bursts with spectroscopic SNe. Bromberg collapsar
probabilities from T90 alone (Cano+2014): **99.995 ± 0.002 % (BAT), 99.487 ± 0.358 % (GBM)**.

**Cosmology / rates / multimessenger (`module-future; cosmo|mm`).**
Wang H.+2024 use it in a Fermi-LGRB Amati-relation cosmology fit; Amelino-Camelia+2026 list it in
a GRB-neutrino quantum-gravity redshift sample (`GRB130215A 43.503 13.395 56338.063 0.597 MP`);
Dong+2022 in an LGRB luminosity function / event-rate study; Graham+2016 in the absolute LGRB
formation rate; Hasan+2024/2025 and Shirokov+2017 in redshift-distribution / spatial studies;
Palliyaguru+2021 searched (and did not find) repeating FRBs from its position;
Aimuratov+2023 and Ruffini+2023 place it in the BdHN classification.
