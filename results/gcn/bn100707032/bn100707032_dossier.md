### High energy (LAT) — a detection our data cannot use
GCN 10945 verbatim: *"This burst was at an angle of 90 degrees to the LAT boresight, which means that
**neither directional nor energy information can be obtained with the standard analysis procedures**.
… Over 300 counts above background were detected within a 5 s interval in coincidence with the main
GBM peak. The significance of this excess was greater than 14 sigma. A preliminary study … suggests
that these events are likely to be low energy gamma-rays, with energies **less than 200 MeV**."*
Corroborated in the refereed literature by **Rubtsov+2012** (§6), whose standard-analysis pipeline
*failed* on this burst for exactly this reason. `results/grb_sample.ecsv` has `HAS_LAT = False`; no
`gll_*` or `LAT/` files exist on disk (§7). **Consequence for us: this burst is GBM-only by
construction, and the 3.7σ "LAT detection" quoted by Basak & Rao 2014 must not be treated as a
usable high-energy constraint.**

## 5. What the literature *wants* this burst to be (bias disclosure for P3)

Every dedicated analysis of GRB 100707A reads it as a **photosphere-dominated** burst:
Burgess+2014 (bright BB), Iyyani+2016 (Γ > 1000), Li+2019 (largest thermal-ratio variation in their
sample), Li+2020 (hybrid jet), Acuner+2020 (**P = 1.00**, ln Z ratio = 1013 — the most photospheric
burst in their sample). Basak & Rao 2014 independently find mean α = **+0.013 ± 0.017**, above the
synchrotron death line in 16/18 bins. This is a **strongly primed** burst: a blind fit that finds a
significant BB is *not* independent confirmation, and a blind fit that does **not** find one is the
more informative outcome. Disclosed here so the P3 diff can be read honestly.
