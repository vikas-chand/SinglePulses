YES — our "no block requires extra spectral structure" is CONSISTENT with, and in fact independently corroborated by, the best-matched literature. The corroboration is strong because Yu, Dereli-Begue & Ryde 2019 (2019ApJ...886...20Y) ran essentially our analysis — same detectors (n6n7n9b1), same 3ML Bayesian-block binning, CPL vs BAND, information criterion — and found CPL preferred in every one of the 8 non-pathological bins of bn081224887 (dDIC = +22.6, +5.5, +1.9, +2.1, +0.3, +1.8, +2.4, +1.1). The two negative bins carry pDIC_BAND = -109 and -240404, which Yu explicitly names as the artifact signature. Bissaldi et al. 2011's time-integrated GBM catalog also selects Comp (CPL) over Band, and Meng 2019 (via Yu 2016) reports COMP as the best-fit model throughout the early burst. Our dAICsimp = 0.0 in six of nine blocks is the same statement.

The apparent counter-evidence does not survive frame alignment. The "081224 requires a blackbody" claim traces to a single lineage — Burgess et al. 2014 -> Iyyani et al. 2016 -> Li 2019/2020 -> Li & Zhang 2021 — and in that lineage the >=10 C-stat improvement is measured against a SYNCHROTRON-ONLY continuum whose low-energy index is pinned near -2/3. GRB 081224's alpha is harder than -2/3 in 11 of 15 bins (Basak & Rao 2014, mean -0.43+/-0.04), so a synchrotron continuum cannot reach the observed hardness and the blackbody is doing the work. Burgess states the mechanism outright: the Band function "has more freedom in the shape below Ep". Where the continuum is empirical, the thermal component evaporates — Li 2019's own CPL+BB table gives thermal flux ratios of 0.09, 0.05, 0.01, 0.01, 0.01 with lower errors that all reach zero, and dAlpha and dEc consistent with zero in all five bins. Basak & Rao's chi2red ratio Band/BBPL = 0.80 says the same thing from the other side.

Two caveats to state in the paper rather than bury. (i) Our result is a null on EXTRA structure, not a null on curvature: every block is well described by a curved continuum with a steep beta (-2.4 to -4.5), matching Yu 2019's -3.3 to -4.3. (ii) Our largest structure hint, blk3 [1.896, 4.382] s with CPLBB, kT = 44.8 keV, LRT = 7.7, dAICsimp = 3.6, falls in exactly the interval where Li 2019 reports the strongest thermal signature (bin 3.69-4.65 s, kT = 26.9 keV, largest |dDIC|, highest flux ratio) and where Burgess 2014 places the photosphere. The honest sentence is "sub-threshold at our STRONG cut, co-located with the published photospheric claim", not "nothing there".

## P4/P5 — ATTRIBUTION (we-wrong / they-wrong / frame)

1. Extra structure: our "0 decisive / 0 strong" vs Burgess 2014 + Iyyani 2016 "significant and strong blackbody" — FRAME-DIFFERENCE that hardens into THEY-wrong. The published significance is dC-stat > 10 measured against a SYNCHROTRON-ONLY continuum, not against Band/CPL. Burgess himself writes: "There is sometimes a much larger change in C-stat between fits with synchrotron and synchrotron+blackbody than those of Band and Band+blackbody owing to the fact that the Band function has more freedom in the shape below Ep", and "When Band alpha was much harder than zero, the synchrotron fit was poor and typically required adding blackbody to fit the data." GRB 081224's alpha is harder than the synchrotron limit -2/3 in 11 of 15 bins (Basak & Rao 2014), mean -0.43+/-0.04. So the BB is repairing a continuum that was forced to asymptote to -2/3. Our menu is empirical, so no BB is demanded — the correct expectation, not a miss. The "THEY-wrong" half is the propagation: Li 2019 Table 1 lists "Best Model = Band+BB" for 081224 citing Iyyani 2016, but Iyyani 2016 never fitted Band+BB for this burst — Li's own CPL+BB fits give thermal ratios 0.01-0.09 all consistent with zero.

2. Model selection (Band vs CPL): our BAND winner with dAIC2 = 0.9 and dAICsimp = 0.0 vs Bissaldi 2011's Comp and Yu 2019's 8/10 positive dDIC — NOT a difference. Our dAIC2 = 0.9 IS the statement "Band and CPL are tied". Frame-consistent.

3. Li 2019's negative dDIC in all 5 bins (nominally favouring CPL+BB) vs our null — FRAME-DIFFERENCE plus a criterion pathology. Li uses DIC on a Bayesian fit; Yu 2019 (same group, same pipeline family) documents that DIC on these fits produces |dDIC| "as large as hundreds of thousand" with pDIC << 0, and warns "It is of course very dangerous to blindly believe such a number". Li's dDIC = -378.3 for a BB carrying 5(+15,-6)% of the flux is that pathology. AIC on maximum-likelihood fits (ours) does not have this failure mode. Do NOT treat this as tension.

4. blk0 Ep = 2141 keV vs Yu 2019 1282(+183,-405) and Shao 2017 1233+/-414 — WE-wrong (soft), or at best frame. Our block is [-0.161, 0.250] s, i.e. 0.41 s and starting 0.16 s BEFORE trigger, versus their 0.35 s / 0.38 s windows starting at T0. Yu's own S for that bin is 14.01, below their S>=20 quality cut. Our Ep sits above their upper error bar; this is an under-constrained Ep on the rising edge, exactly the regime where our L9 Ep-cap/BOUND_CAPPED diagnosis is needed. Flag blk0 Ep as not a measurement.

5. blk3 kT = 44.8 keV with LRT = 7.7 (our largest BB hint) vs Li 2019's [3.69,4.65] kT = 26.9(+9.7,-16.0) keV with the most negative dDIC and highest thermal ratio — AGREEMENT, not a difference. Both find the strongest BB hint in the same ~2-5 s window; ours simply does not clear our STRONG threshold (dAICsimp 3.6 < 6). Report it as a sub-threshold hint that lands where the literature puts the photosphere, not as a null.

6. T90 = 20.05 s vs GBM catalog 16.45+/-1.16 s — WE-wrong. Our T90_STOP (19.79 s on disk) sits at the source-window edge (20.165 s) and the on-disk T90_ERR is 47.0 s, larger than the value. The measurement is window-truncated and its error is meaningless. The spread among published values (Qin 9.41 in 8-1000 keV, WAM 10.3 in 50 keV-5 MeV, GBM 16.45 in 50-300 keV) is a genuine FRAME-difference driven by band and background choice, but ours is outside that family for a pipeline reason, not a physics reason.

7. Lag +0.816 s vs Maraventano tau12 = 1.24+/-0.12 s and Lu 2018 tau31 = 1.47+/-0.11 s — FRAME-DIFFERENCE (channel-pair definition and CCF estimator). Same sign, same order. But an internal WE-wrong is exposed: our own results/temporal_catalog_human.ecsv carries LAG_S = -0.6162 for this burst while the blind run reports lag_std = +0.816. Two of our products disagree on the SIGN of the lag. Both cannot be right; the literature (three independent papers, all positive) says the sign is positive.

8. Basak & Rao 2014's "LAT detection 3.1 sigma" for 081224 attributed to Ackermann et al. 2013 — THEY-wrong. Ackermann 2013 explicitly excludes 081224 because on-ground analysis did not confirm the excess and a retraction was issued. Maraventano 2025's LLE table is blank for this burst. Do not carry a LAT/LLE detection for 081224 into our tables on Basak & Rao's authority.

9. Suzaku GCN 8739 fluence "2.87(+0.09,-0.04) erg/cm^2" — THEY-wrong (typo): the exponent x10^-5 is missing. Consistent with GBM's (3.76+/-0.02)e-5 erg/cm2 over a wider band. Quote it as published and flag, do not silently correct.

## LESSONS → skills files

- L10 (Ep-vs-Ec trap): Li 2019 and Yu 2019 tabulate 'Ec' = the CPL e-folding energy, not Epeak. Ep = (2+alpha)*Ec, verified on Yu 2019 Table 6 where both columns appear (alpha=-0.13, Ec=344.76 -> Ep=644.75). Add a frame-alignment gate to SpectralFitting.md: before diffing any Ep, resolve whether the published column is nuFnu-peak or e-folding, and record which. Naively diffing our Ep=724 against Li's Ec=342.6 would have manufactured a factor-1.9 'tension'.

- L11 (attribute BB claims to the continuum, not the burst): a published 'this burst requires a blackbody' is a statement about the CONTINUUM it was measured against. Burgess 2014 (2014ApJ...784...17B) states verbatim that dC-stat(synchrotron -> synchrotron+BB) far exceeds dC-stat(Band -> Band+BB) 'owing to the fact that the Band function has more freedom in the shape below Ep', and that hard Band alpha is what forces the BB into synchrotron fits. Rule: when a paper reports an extra component, extract (i) the base continuum, (ii) the statistic, (iii) the threshold, BEFORE recording it as a diff against our empirical menu. Our empirical null and their synchrotron+BB are not in conflict.

- L12 (citation-chain audit): Li 2019 Table 1 lists 'Best Model = Band+BB' for 081224887 citing Iyyani et al. (2016), but Iyyani 2016 fitted synchrotron+BB, never Band+BB, for this burst. Separately, Basak & Rao 2014 credit Ackermann et al. 2013 with a 3.1-sigma LAT detection of 081224, while Ackermann 2013 explicitly excludes the burst and cites the GCN 8726 retraction. Always open the CITED paper, not the citing table, before importing a 'best model' or a detection into our records.

- L13 (free binning validation): Li & Zhang 2021 (2021ApJS..253...43L) publish Bayesian-block counts and edges for many of our bursts. For bn081224887 they report Ntot = 9 blocks and quote edges 1.896, 5.424, 12.502 s, all of which appear in our 9 blocks to 3 decimals. Add a Stage-2 QC step to the reconciliation protocol: cross-check our block edges against Li & Zhang 2021 Tables 1/3, Yu 2019 Tables 3-40, and Shao 2017 Table 2 wherever the burst overlaps. It costs one grep and turns binning from an assumption into a verified input.

- L14 (do not report a window-truncated T90): our T90 = 20.05 s for bn081224887 against a source window of [-1.280, 20.165] s, with on-disk T90_STOP = 19.79 and T90_ERR = 47.0 s (error larger than the value). Published T90 is 16.45+/-1.16 s (GBM catalog, quoted identically by Li 2019, Li & Zhang 2021, Shao 2017). Add a hard QC flag: if T90_STOP is within ~1 s of the source-window edge, or T90_ERR > T90, emit T90_WINDOW_LIMITED and suppress the value from tables.

- L15 (internal sign-convention conflict on spectral lag): results/temporal_catalog_human.ecsv carries LAG_S = -0.6162 s for bn081224887 while the blind run reports lag_std = +0.816 s. Three independent papers give a positive lag (Maraventano 2025 tau12 = 1.24+/-0.12 s, tau13 = 1.97+/-0.17 s, tau14 = 2.57+/-0.34 s; Lu 2018 tau31 = 1.47+/-0.11 s), so the sign is positive (soft lags hard). Fix the convention in one place, add a regression test with 081224 as the anchor, and always state the channel pair (ours is not directly comparable to tau12/tau13/tau14 until the bands are declared).

- L16 (railed/frozen beta in the literature is INCOMPARABLE, not a diff): Lu 2012 rails beta at -4.57 in 2 of 12 bins of 081224 and Lu 2018 freezes beta at -2.56 in one bin, exactly as our -5.00 bound rails. Extend the L9/BOUND_CAPPED rule to the comparison side: detect the literature's own bound (repeated identical value, or +/-0.00 errors) and mark the pair INCOMPARABLE rather than scoring a difference. All our 081224 betas were free, which is worth stating as a strength.

- L17 (report co-located sub-threshold hints, do not flatten them to zero): our single largest structure hint (blk3 [1.896,4.382] s, CPLBB, kT = 44.8 keV, LRT = 7.7, dAICsimp = 3.6) lands in the same interval as Li 2019's strongest thermal bin (3.69-4.65 s, kT = 26.9 keV, largest |dDIC|, highest flux ratio) and Burgess 2014's photosphere. A census that only reports counts above STRONG loses this. Add a 'co-located sub-threshold' column so the paper can say 'sub-threshold, but where the literature puts it' instead of 'nothing'.

- L18 (DIC-based literature verdicts need a pathology check before use): Yu 2019 warns in print that |dDIC| can reach 'hundreds of thousand' with pDIC << 0 and that 'It is of course very dangerous to blindly believe such a number'. For 081224887, 2 of 10 bins have dDIC = -105 and -240407 with pDIC_BAND = -109 and -240404. Rule for the reconciliation skill: when diffing against a DIC-based paper, discard any bin whose pDIC is negative or exceeds the parameter count, and never treat a large negative dDIC as evidence without the accompanying parameter table (Li 2019's dDIC = -378.3 accompanies a thermal flux ratio of 0.05(+0.15,-0.06), i.e. zero).
