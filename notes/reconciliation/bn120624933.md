## P6 — VERDICT

VERIFIED — our clean NULL is correct, and the literature agrees with it at frame-matched level on both axes.

Thermal: Chang et al. 2026 (2026MNRAS.546ag047C) is a direct, previously unexploited reconciliation anchor. On Fermi/GBM over 0-20 s, with the same thermal model menu we use, both CPL+BB and Band+BB return unconstrained posteriors ("Non-Con.") and pure Band wins on BIC. They place 120624B in their purely-non-thermal class with a magnetization lower limit sigma > 14.38 and a maximum allowed photospheric kT_max = 30.10 keV. Our LRT < 4 in all 10 blocks, with fitted kT from 1.0 to 24.4 keV — every value below their ceiling. Two different statistical frameworks, same answer.

High-energy structure: de Ugarte Postigo et al. 2013 did the joint GBM+LLE fit and state verbatim that "the spectrum in the LAT energy range is still well described by a Band function and no emerging high-energy extra component is evident." Sharma et al. 2021 tried Band+PL and Band+CPL jointly with LLE and report no extra component. Our dAICsimp = 0.0 in 8/10 blocks is the same conclusion.

The apparent paradox in the key question dissolves entirely on frame alignment, and it dissolves on published numbers, not on argument: Ajello et al. 2019 give T_LAT,0 = 73.7 s, so the first >100 MeV LAT event arrives 47 s after our window closes; within the whole 271-s GBM window there are only four LAT events with P > 0.9, the hardest being 0.36 GeV at t = -201.87 s, in episode 1. The 10 sigma of GCN 13379 is an LLE-band integral over ~T0-250 to ~T0+20 s, ~93% of which precedes our window, taken while the source sat ~70 deg off-axis outside the nominal LAT FoV; the >100 MeV detection is a 1030-s extended component peaking at 380 +/- 20 s, only observable after the ARR slew at T0+100 s. There was never any high-energy signal inside 0-26 s for us to have missed. A NULL was the only correct answer.

Parameter agreement is excellent where frames match and explained where they do not. Our alpha = -1.01 lands on Konus (-1.02), Suzaku (-1.03) and dUP episode 3 (-1.04) to better than 0.05. Our blk 1 Ep = 960 keV matches the Konus maximum-count-rate spectrum (1000 -450/+620 keV) over GBM T0 +1.62 to +9.81 s to under 0.1 sigma — the single cleanest frame-matched check available for this burst, and we pass it. The burst-averaged Ep values of 498-628 keV are diluted by episodes 1-2 and are not our comparator. Our beta = -5.00 railings are corroborated rather than contradicted: Konus also fails to constrain beta at the peak, quoting only an upper limit < -1.8.

Two caveats on the record. First, our T90 = 23.58 s must be published with an explicit multi-episode flag; quoting it against the catalog 271.36 s would look like a factor-11 error when it is a window definition. Second, the one open follow-up is blocks 1 and 2 with LLE included: Ajello puts 90% of the LLE fluence in 5.76-9.12 s, exactly straddling those two blocks, and our blind run was GBM-only (n0, n1, n2, b0). dUP already did the equivalent joint fit and found nothing, so this is a falsifiable prediction we should expect to confirm, not a gap that threatens the result. Secondary open item: our post-burst background starts at 35.0 s while Tak+2023 still fit source out to 37.75 s — worth a sensitivity check, as it is the one plausible contributor to our Ep sitting ~200 keV above Chang's frame-matched 558.8 keV.

Temporal corroboration is a bonus. Tak et al. 2023 independently fit this as a single broad pulse with t_rise = 9.6 s, t_decay = 7.0 s and t_bumps = 0.0 s. Their rise/decay ratio of 1.371 sits within 3% of our phi = 1.415, and both classify the pulse as rise-dominated and non-FRED — unusual against our sample median phi of ~0.19. Confirm our phi convention before claiming this match in print.

## P4/P5 — ATTRIBUTION (we-wrong / they-wrong / frame)

FRAME-difference (dominant, 7 of 9 diffs):
- D1 T90 23.58 s vs 271.36 s — FRAME. GBM triggered on episode 3 of 3 because triggering was disabled at high geomagnetic latitude during episodes 1-2 (GCN 13377 verbatim). Our window is episode 3; the catalog T90 describes a burst we never observed. Zero information content as a diff.
- D2 Ep 764.8 vs 498-628 keV — FRAME, two-part. (a) Four of the five comparators (GBM GCN, Konus, Suzaku, and dUP's inclusion of the pre-trigger rise) average over the softer episodes 1-2; our window is the hardest slice of the burst, so a higher Ep is expected. (b) The residual against the one frame-matched value (Chang 558.8 keV over 0-20 s) sits on the standard Band Ep-beta degeneracy ridge: our softer beta = -2.55 trades against a higher Ep, theirs harder beta = -2.19 against a lower Ep. Direction check confirms this is not a window artifact — extending 20 s to 26.3 s adds our softest block (blk 9, Ep = 473) and should LOWER Ep, so the offset is beta-driven, not window-driven.
- D3 Ep at peak (Konus 1000 vs our 960) — FRAME-agreement, no diff. This is the clean control that proves D2 is a window/degeneracy effect and not a calibration or response error on our side.
- D4 beta = -5.00 railing — FRAME, NOT our bug. Konus, with independent hardware over an overlapping interval, also fails to constrain beta (quotes only < -1.8). Both say the same thing; our BOUND_CAPPED flag is the correct encoding. It must never be reported as a measured beta of -5.
- D5 alpha — FRAME-agreement with Konus/Suzaku/dUP to <0.05; residual vs Chang/GBM-GCN is the alpha-Ep-beta covariance.
- D6 thermal NULL — FRAME-agreement, and the strongest single result here. Chang+2026 ran the identical thermal menu (CPL+BB, Band+BB) on the identical instrument over a matched 0-20 s window and got unconstrained posteriors for both, selecting pure Band. Two different statistics (their BIC/MCMC vs our AIC/LRT) converge. dUP and Tak are INCOMPARABLE on this axis because neither fitted a thermal model — their silence is not evidence.
- D7 no extra high-energy component — FRAME-agreement. dUP+2013 tested exactly this with joint GBM+LLE and found nothing; Sharma+2021 tested Band+PL and Band+CPL jointly with LLE and found nothing. The Band-vs-CPL sub-decision mismatch is FRAME (model menu): Chang has no SBPL in their menu, we do, and SBPL/Band are the same physical statement.
- D8 the 10-sigma LAT detection — FRAME, decisively. Neither WE-wrong nor THEY-wrong: the detection is real and our NULL is real, and they are statements about disjoint time intervals. T_LAT,0 = 73.7 s > our window end 26.343 s. The 10 sigma is an LLE-band integral dominated by pre-trigger episodes 1-2, and the >100 MeV signal is a 1030-s extended component peaking at 380 s. There was never a prediction of high-energy structure inside 0-26 s to test.
- D10 pulse asymmetry — FRAME-agreement (our phi 1.415 vs Tak's t_rise/t_decay 1.371), CONDITIONAL on confirming our phi convention. Both independently classify this as a rise-dominated, non-FRED single pulse.

WE-wrong (candidate, unconfirmed):
- D11 only. Our post-burst background window opens at 35.0 s while Tak+2023 still fit source counts to 37.75 s. Possible mild over-subtraction of the pulse tail, hardening the spectrum and contributing to the residual part of D2. This is the one line item that could be a genuine defect on our side, and it is second-order (affects parameter values, not a single model decision).

THEY-wrong: none. No published result for this burst is an artifact of a forced choice. The nearest thing to a defect is Ajello+2019's tab_durations, where T_GBM,95 - T_GBM,05 = 274.34 s but T_GBM,90 is listed as the catalog 271.36 s, and where their T_LLE,05/95 = 5.76/9.12 s appears to disagree with dUP's statement that all three peaks are visible in LLE. Both are internal to the literature and do not touch our comparison.

INCOMPARABLE (explicitly not tension): Swift-BAT's 15-150 keV power-law index 1.17 (band too narrow to constrain Ep or beta); our MVT = 0.590 s (limit) and lag_std = +0.962 s (no published counterpart exists); dUP's and Tak's silence on thermal components (never tested); Sharma+2021's per-burst Band parameters (not published, only bolometric fluence and Eiso).

## LESSONS → skills files

- TRIGGER TIME IS NOT BURST START. GRB 120624B's GBM trigger fired on the third of three episodes because triggering was disabled at high geomagnetic latitude during episodes 1-2 (GCN 13377, verbatim). Our window saw ~10% of the burst while the catalog T90 = 271.36 s describes all of it. Add a MULTI_EPISODE / TRIGGER_TRUNCATED flag fired automatically when catalog T90 exceeds our analysis window by more than ~3x, and never publish our T90 for such bursts without it. Ajello+2019's T_GBM,05 column (-260 s here) is a cheap machine-readable detector for this condition.

- A LAT OR LLE DETECTION IS A STATEMENT ABOUT AN INTERVAL, NOT ABOUT A BURST. Before treating a LAT detection as predicting extra spectral structure in our blocks, look up T_LAT,0 in Ajello+2019 tab_durations. Here T_LAT,0 = 73.7 s versus our window end of 26.343 s: there was literally nothing to find. Make this a standing pre-check in the reconciliation protocol, so a 10-sigma headline never again reads as a challenge to a NULL it cannot address.

- beta = -5.00 RAILING IS NOT AUTOMATICALLY OUR BUG. Konus-Wind, independent hardware over an overlapping interval, also failed to constrain beta at the peak and published only an upper limit (< -1.8). Before charging a BOUND_CAPPED beta as a fit failure, check whether ANY instrument constrained beta at that epoch. When none did, the railing is the correct encoding of a real observational limit and should be reported as BOUND_CAPPED, never as a measured value.

- dAICsimp = 0.0 IS AMBIGUOUS WHEN THE WINNER IS ITSELF A SIMPLE MODEL. Our blk -1 reports dAICsimp = 0.0 with SBPL winning, which is trivially zero by construction and says nothing about Band versus CPL. Chang+2026 report Band over CPL at dBIC = 15 (dAIC ~19, DECISIVE) on the same data. We need a separate, explicit Band-vs-CPL margin in the summary line, otherwise our output cannot be compared to the model choice the literature actually publishes.

- FOR MULTI-EPISODE BURSTS, THE GCN BURST-AVERAGED Ep IS THE WRONG COMPARATOR. Our Ep = 764.8 keV looked 2.7-4.5 sigma discrepant against four published values of 498-628 keV until we located Konus's maximum-count-rate spectrum, converted its T0 (GBM T0 - 228.02 s), and found it lands at GBM T0 +1.618 to +9.808 s inside our blocks, giving Ep = 1000 (-450,+620) keV against our 960. Konus always publishes a peak-interval spectrum alongside the integrated one; always convert its T0 and use it. Standing rule: convert every instrument's T0 to the GBM frame BEFORE diffing anything.

- SEARCH ADS FULL TEXT FOR THE BURST NAME; DO NOT STOP AT THE DEDICATED PAPER. The dedicated paper (de Ugarte Postigo+2013) fitted Band only and could not speak to our thermal question at all. The decisive anchor was Chang+2026 (arXiv:2601.04468), a population paper that ran our exact Band+BB / CPL+BB menu on a matched 0-20 s window and reported 'Non-Con.' for both. Population papers with model-comparison tables are stronger reconciliation anchors than discovery papers, and they are only found by full-text search.

- DISTINGUISH CORROBORATION FROM SILENCE. de Ugarte Postigo+2013 (Band only) and Tak+2023 (explicitly single-component PL/CPL/Band) never tested a thermal model, so they are INCOMPARABLE on our thermal NULL, not supporting evidence. Only Chang+2026, which actually ran the thermal models, corroborates. Recording 'they did not report a blackbody' as agreement would have manufactured a false consensus from three sources where only one exists.

- CHECK THAT OUR BACKGROUND WINDOWS DO NOT OVERLAP INTERVALS OTHERS STILL FIT AS SOURCE. Our post-burst background opens at 35.0 s while Tak+2023 fit source counts out to 37.75 s, and our pre-burst window closes at -8.0 s within 0.2 s of dUP's published episode-3 onset at -8.19 s. Cross-checking our background edges against published pulse extents is a fast, cheap audit that should run for every reconciliation burst, since tail over-subtraction hardens the spectrum and propagates straight into Ep.

- CROSS-VALIDATE A NEW LITERATURE SOURCE ON A BURST WE HAVE ALREADY RECONCILED. Chang+2026's table independently lists 110721A as Band+BB, matching our own completed reconciliation record for that burst. Confirming a new source on known ground before trusting it on unknown ground is a cheap and powerful reliability gate; adopt it as standard practice.
