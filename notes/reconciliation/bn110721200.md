## P6 — VERDICT

(a) YES — our blk2 kT=40.9 keV IS the published photospheric component, but the attribution belongs to Axelsson, not Guiriec. Axelsson's Band+BB gives kT = 39 (+4.0/-3.7) keV over [0.83,1.54] s and 35 (+3.4/-3.5) keV over [1.54,2.18] s; our blk2 [0.470,1.955] spans both and returns 40.9 keV — agreement at <0.5 sigma. The significance also matches quantitatively, not just in sign: their C-stat improvement summed over the same span is 45 + 31 = 76 (2 dof each), our LRT is 84.8. By their own Monte-Carlo calibration ("dC-stat = 30 ... corresponds to a 10^-7 probability of not having a real blackbody"), a 76-84 improvement is far beyond that. So the strongest "extra structure needed" margin in our whole campaign (dAICsimp = 81.2, 4e17:1) is not an anomaly to be defended — it is a blind, independent replication of the canonical photosphere detection, obtained with different code, different binning (Bayesian Blocks vs SNR-40; Axelsson checked a Bayesian-Blocks binning too and found it "fully consistent"), and a wider model menu. That is the single most valuable result in this reconciliation and should be the campaign's headline validation case. Guiriec+2013 (2013ApJ...770...32G) is an analysis of the SHORT GRB 120323A and mentions 110721A only as an analogue, crediting the BB detection to Axelsson et al. 2012 — do not cite it as the source of this burst's photosphere.

(b) NO — our result cannot discriminate synchrotron from photosphere, and our own margins say so. Three reasons, in order of force. FIRST, the brief's framing is wrong: there is no Zhang+2016 synchrotron claim for this burst (2016ApJ...816...72Z is GRB 130606B). The real counter-claim, Zhang, Bing et al. 2012 (2012ApJ...758L..34Z), is not an either/or — it explicitly states "We confirm the conclusion of Axelsson et al. (2012) that adding a shoulder thermal component can improve the fits to the data significantly", and disputes only whether the BAND component is photospheric. The published picture is already a both/and: subdominant BB = photosphere, dominant Band = optically-thin non-thermal. Our result is that same both/and (a BB decaying 40.9 -> 29.3 -> 22.5 -> 13.3 -> 6.5 keV and switching off at ~6 s, riding a continuum whose peak sweeps 19 MeV -> 270 keV), so we corroborate the structure both camps assume rather than adjudicating between them. SECOND, the actual discriminator in Zhang+2012 is the burst's position relative to the photosphere "death line" in the REST-FRAME Ep-L plane, and our redshift is ambiguous by a factor 3.3 in (1+z): z = 0.382 or 3.512 (GCN 12193, with no confirming lines at either). Iyyani+2013 gave up and adopted z = 2. We cannot compute a rest-frame Ep or luminosity, so we cannot re-derive the death-line test; we can only cite Zhang+2012's finding that the burst clears it for BOTH candidate redshifts. Corollary for the paper: GRB 110721A must be EXCLUDED from every rest-frame correlation (Amati, Yonetoku, Ep-kT-L). THIRD, the AIC margins the question invokes are self-refuting as physics discriminators. SBPL+BB vs 2SBPL/DSBPL alternates block to block — blk2 dAIC2 = 0.4, blk3 = 8.6 (DSBPL), blk4 = 6.8 (DSBPL), blk5 = 0.8, blk6 = 1.8 — so only blk3 and blk4 clear our own STRONG threshold (>=6), and they select the opposite physics from blk2. No emission mechanism switches between "thermal component" and "double break" on 0.7 s timescales while alpha, beta, Ep and kT all evolve smoothly AND reproduce Axelsson's published values. The alternation is a model degeneracy: a blackbody at kT ~ 20-40 keV and a second SBPL break near ~3kT occupy the same decade of the spectrum and are not separable at GBM resolution. The correct report for blk2-blk6 is DEGENERATE (SBPL+BB == 2SBPL), and dAIC2 must never be read as physics when the two competitors are this degenerate — only dAICsimp ("is extra structure needed") survives, and it answers YES emphatically.

WHAT WE CAN LEGITIMATELY CLAIM. (1) A blind four-parameter reproduction of Axelsson Table 1 bin [2.18,2.75]: Ep 269.0 vs 269 (+19/-22), alpha -0.70 vs -0.68, beta -2.38 vs -2.37, kT 29.3 vs 30 +/- 1.9. (2) Independent recovery of the record-extreme early peak, Ep = 19.1 MeV, matching Zhang+2012's independent Band-only 19.56 +/- 4.22 MeV to 0.1 sigma. (3) Independent confirmation that the thermal component turns off at ~6 s. (4) alpha above the fast-cooling synchrotron limit throughout the bright phase (-0.92, -0.88, -0.70), which is Axelsson's and Iyyani's stated constraint on standard optically-thin synchrotron. (5) Our own beta = -2.26 correcting the -1.77 in both GCN circulars, backed by GCN 12188 and by all eight of Axelsson's time-resolved bins.

THE ONE NON-CIRCULAR NEXT STEP. No published analysis of this burst has ever fit a 2SBPL/low-energy-break model (Axelsson: Band, Band+BB, Band+mBB; Zhang+2012: Band; Iyyani: BB+Band). 2SBPL is the SYNCHROTRON parameterisation. The discriminating measurement is therefore not "which model wins on AIC" but whether the DSBPL low-energy index pair in blk3/blk4 lands on the synchrotron values (-2/3 above the cooling break, -3/2 below) with a physically sensible break ratio, or on values with no synchrotron meaning. Those indices are not in the blind result and should be extracted; that is a real contribution to the debate. It must wait until the blk9 regression and the blk1 LRT=-0.0 optimizer failure are fixed, because both live in the same Band+BB / composite-fit machinery.

BLOCKING BEFORE PUBLICATION: fix the blk9 bound-widening regression; fix or hard-error the negative nested LRT; mask kT where LRT <= 2; recompute T90 on a window >= 2x the expected duration; exclude this burst from rest-frame correlations.

- L-A (promote to regression test): blk3 reproduces four independent published parameters simultaneously — Ep 269.0 vs 269(+19/-22), alpha -0.70 vs -0.68, beta -2.38 vs -2.37, kT 29.3 vs 30+/-1.9 (Axelsson 2012 Table 1, bin 2.18-2.75 s). - L-B (NEW, extends L9 → SpectralFitting.md): a bound-widening fix must be re-validated on the FAINTEST blocks, not only the block that motivated it. L9 (widen the 5 MeV Ep cap) correctly un-railed blk0 but REGRESSED blk9 from alpha=-0.93 / Ep=373 keV (all 12 archival runs) to alpha=+0.82 / Ep=42.3 keV. Rule: every bound change requires a before/after diff over ALL blocks of the burst, with a hard alarm on any block whose alpha crosses the synchrotron death line -2/3, or whose Ep moves by more than 3 sigma, or whose winner changes family.

- L-C (NEW): a nested-model LRT that is zero or negative is mathematically impossible and must raise an error, never be reported as a non-detection. blk1 has Band+BB LRT = -0.0 while the WINNING model in the same block is SBPL+BB with kT=23.3 — proving the Band+BB fit failed rather than that the blackbody is absent. Cross-check every nested LRT against whether the component appears in any winning composite model.

- L-D (NEW): kT is only a measurement where the BB is significant. Mask kT from all kT(t) tracks and Ep-kT correlations where LRT <= 2 (our blk8 kT=33.1 at LRT=0.6 would fake a late thermal rebrightening from 4.4 keV; blk0 kT=2.7 at LRT=1.5 likewise). Adopt Axelsson's published convention explicitly: they print the unconstrained bin as 4.9_unc and use filled/open symbols for 5-sigma/3-sigma BB significance.

- L-E (NEW, false corroboration): two independent instruments agreeing is not independent verification when they share the analysis primitive. GBM (GCN 12187) and Konus-Wind (GCN 12191) both report beta = -1.77 — apparent cross-instrument confirmation — but both are single time-integrated Band fits to a spectrum whose Ep sweeps 19 MeV -> 0.3 MeV, and the Fermi-LAT circular (GCN 12188) contradicts both with -2.9+/-0.4. Our -2.26 is right. Never treat time-integrated circular betas as a check on a high-energy index for a burst with strong hard-to-soft evolution.
