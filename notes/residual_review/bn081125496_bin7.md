## Comparative paragraph

The four simple continua are visually indistinguishable in this bin: the SED curves
overlap over the entire plotted 10-110 keV range and the NA residual strips are the
same flat, sub-sigma pattern in every panel. The stored statistics agree — the total
-2lnL spread across BAND/CPL/SBPL/DSBPL is 0.33 (1671.46 to 1671.79), so no model
absorbs any structure the others show, because there is no structure to absorb: the
data are featureless at this significance over the only band with detections
(NA, ~13-90 keV), with a peak at ~75-90 keV (EPK_CURVE 74.6-88.3 across models,
mutually consistent). Every parameter beyond {low-energy slope, peak} is
bound-or-edge dominated: Band's beta hits the -5 bound from below, SBPL's
Ebreak/beta are edge-of-data, and DSBPL's XB spans its full allowed range. CPL — the
one model whose parameters are all data-constrained — is the right description, and
its selection is a pure parsimony call, not a residual-driven one. A tie on residuals
is a tie; the models disagree only in how many unconstrained parameters they carry.
One model-independent fit-health item to escalate: **EAC(nb) and EAC(b1) are railed at
exactly 0.80 (the lower bound) in all four models** — the fits want the nb/b1
normalizations pushed down by at least 20% relative to na. This is invisible in the
residual strips (nb/b1 render no points) and identical across models, so it is a
cross-detector calibration/selection question for this block, not evidence about the
spectral shape.
