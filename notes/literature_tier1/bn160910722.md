# bn160910722 — GRB 160910A (literature candidates)

## Chattopadhyay et al. 2019, ApJ, 884, 123 (AstroSat CZTI prompt-emission polarimetry of 11 first-year GRBs)
- id: arXiv:1707.06595
- models: Band (GBM fit for the polarimetry interval); preliminary Band + blackbody (spectro-polarimetric follow-up flagged as in progress)
- findings: Fits the main pulse (5.9-10.4 s) with a Band function with a very hard alpha = -0.36+/-0.03, beta = -2.38, Ep = 330+/-13 keV, and measures a high polarization fraction of 94+/-32% (PA ~44 deg, >=3-sigma detection, chance probability <0.1%). Their preliminary spectral analysis finds a deviation from the Band model requiring an additional thermal blackbody for four bursts including 160910A, and calls 160910A (with 160106A, 160509A) 'peculiar' because the required blackbody attains a temperature HIGHER than the burst's Epeak.

## von Kienlin et al. 2020, ApJ, 893, 46 (The Fourth Fermi-GBM Gamma-Ray Burst Catalog: A Decade of Data)
- id: arXiv:2002.11460
- models: Catalog entry (duration/fluence/peak-flux; spectral fits deferred to the companion spectral catalog)
- findings: Catalog row for bn160910722 = GRB 160910A: T90 = 24.320+/-0.362 s (starting at T0+4.6 s), T50 = 3.584 s, 10-1000 keV fluence 7.97e-5 erg/cm2, 64-ms peak photon flux ~82 ph/s/cm2 — one of the brightest triggers in the era, with flags for Konus, INTEGRAL, LAT, AstroSat, CALET, Swift-XRT follow-up and a GBM Autonomous Repoint Request.

## Poolakkil et al. 2021, ApJ, 913, 60 (The Fermi GBM Gamma-Ray Burst Spectral Catalog: 10 Years of Data)
- id: arXiv:2103.13528
- models: Standard GBM catalog four-model suite: Band, Comptonized (CPL), smoothly-broken power law, power law (time-integrated fluence + peak-flux spectra)
- findings: The burst's row is in the machine-readable catalog (verified via the HEASARC fermigbrst table, which hosts this catalog's fits; the arXiv PDF prints only excerpt rows): the fluence spectrum is best fit by a BAND function with Ep = 321.9 keV, alpha = -0.805, beta = -2.17. Time-integrated values agree with the GCN 19901 GBM values (Ep = 347+/-9 keV, alpha = -0.78, beta = -2.23) — CANDIDATE row, re-verify against the published machine-readable table.

## Ajello et al. 2019, ApJ, 878, 52 (A Decade of Gamma-Ray Bursts Observed by Fermi-LAT: The Second GRB Catalog, 2FLGC)
- id: arXiv:1906.11403
- models: Power law for the LAT >100 MeV likelihood analysis (plus LLE detection); catalog joint GBM/LAT time-domain characterization
- findings: GRB 160910A is in the 2FLGC with an LLE detection flag and LAT emission: LAT onset ~4.6 s after the GBM trigger, and the 0-500 s likelihood fit gives 14 events, TS = 44, with a soft photon index of -2.5+/-0.5; highest-energy events are only ~0.2 GeV class (MPE summary quotes a 500 MeV photon at ~9 ks). LAT emission is weak/soft compared with the very bright GBM pulse.

## Wang, Xie, Gao, Xiao, Dong, Zhang & Zhi 2024, RAA, 24(2) (Time-resolved Spectral Properties of Fermi-GBM Bright Long Gamma-Ray Bursts)
- id: arXiv:2401.14969
- models: Band ('Band-shape') function fitted to time-resolved bins (78 bright long GRBs, 1490 spectra)
- findings: bn160910722 is in their 78-burst sample (detectors n1, n2, (n5), b0; source interval 0-40 s; 20 time-resolved spectra, 18 usable; listed as a single-pulse burst). Their pattern tables classify this burst's Ep evolution as hard-to-soft and its alpha evolution as intensity-tracking, with a positive flux-Ep correlation (type 2p, r = 0.79) — column identification of Ep vs alpha is a CANDIDATE reading, verify against their Table definitions. Sample-wide they conclude the diversity of patterns implies more than one radiation mechanism (photospheric + synchrotron).

## Relevance to our program
GRB 160910A is a bright (fluence ~8e-5 erg/cm2), hard-alpha single-pulse burst in our sample, and the literature touches several of our questions directly: (1) blackbody/second component — Chattopadhyay+2019 claim a Band+BB improvement with the peculiar feature kT > Ep, i.e. a thermal component ABOVE the νFν peak rather than the usual low-energy shoulder, which bears on our Band+BB/CPL+BB fits, the Ep-kT (Burgess) relation, and the saddle/second-hump question; (2) low-energy break/synchrotron — the time-integrated alpha (-0.36 to -0.8 depending on interval) sits at or above the synchrotron line of death, making our 2SBPL/DSBPL low-energy-break search on this burst novel (no published two-break or low-energy-break study exists for it); (3) spectral evolution — Wang+2024 report hard-to-soft Ep with intensity-tracking alpha and a positive F-Ep correlation, directly comparable to our rise-phase IT/HTS classification; (4) high-energy behavior — 2FLGC reports weak, soft (index -2.5) LAT/LLE emission, relevant to any high-energy cutoff constraint. No dedicated single-burst spectroscopy paper exists; coverage is catalog rows plus the CZTI polarimetry paper (where the 94% polarization + hard alpha combination is used to argue for non-standard emission), so our time-resolved two-break analysis would be the first dedicated spectral study.
