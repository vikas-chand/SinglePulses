# bn130427324 — GRB 130427A (literature candidates)

## Preece et al. 2014, Science 343, 51
- id: arXiv:1311.5581 / 2014Sci...343...51P
- models: Time-resolved Band fits at fine (10s-of-ms) resolution; synchrotron photon model + blackbody (search snippet: extra BB required at 4-sigma in the onset pulse)
- findings: Fine time-resolved GBM spectroscopy of the first ~2.5 s pulse (possibly the brightest well-isolated pulse ever observed): Ep shows power-law decays from pulse onset consistent with internal synchrotron-shock models, but a strongly correlated luminosity-Ep power law is inconsistent with curvature effects; no existing model accounts for all spectral and temporal behavior simultaneously.

## Ackermann et al. 2014, Science 343, 42
- id: arXiv:1311.5623 / 2014Sci...343...42A
- models: Time-resolved GBM+LAT fits (Band + extra hard power-law component)
- findings: Reports >100 MeV emission up to 95 GeV lasting ~20 h; time-resolved fits show strong evidence for an extra hard spectral component above a few GeV in the extended emission, challenging the external-shock synchrotron interpretation and possibly the first clear evidence of afterglow inverse-Compton emission.

## Maselli et al. 2014, Science 343, 48
- id: arXiv:1311.5254 / 2014Sci...343...48M
- models: Multiwavelength (Swift + ground-based) prompt-to-afterglow SED evolution; not a dedicated GBM model comparison
- findings: Comprehensive multiwavelength view of the burst with the highest gamma-ray fluence observed (z=0.34, L~3e53 erg/s); properties similar to the most luminous high-z GRBs, arguing for a common central engine across the full range of GRB isotropic energies. Peripheral to prompt spectroscopy but sets the burst's context.

## Ravasio et al. 2019, A&A 625, A60
- id: arXiv:1903.02555 / 2019A&A...625A..60R
- models: 2SBPL (double smoothly broken power law) vs Band/SBPL on the 10 brightest Fermi long GRBs
- findings: The flagship low-energy-break sample: 8/10 brightest long GRBs show a break below Ep with slopes consistent with marginally fast-cooling synchrotron. Critically for us, GRB 130427A was EXCLUDED because pulse pile-up deformed the spectrum — standard spectral analysis was possible only on its precursor, which failed their selection criteria.

## Liang, Ruffini, Rueda, Moradi, Wang & Xue 2023, ApJ 945, 10
- id: arXiv:1910.12615 / 2023ApJ...945...10L
- models: Cutoff power law (SN-rise episode), CPL + blackbody (ultrarelativistic prompt-emission phase), featureless spectrum (cavity; CPL with alpha=-1.52, Ec~496 keV at ~15-25.5 s)
- findings: Time-resolved spectral analysis of 4 bright bursts including 130427A within the BdHN framework: identifies three episodes (SN-rise, UPE with CPL+BB, cavity) and self-similar structures / power laws in the UPE-phase time-resolved spectra common to all BdHN I. Also notes GBM count rate exceeded ~8e4 counts/s at T0+3.4-8.6 s with pile-up significantly deforming the spectrum.

## Du, Peng, Chen, Li & Yin 2022, ApJ (Nov 2022)
- id: DOI 10.3847/1538-4357/ac94d2
- models: Bayesian time-resolved spectral analysis; thermal (blackbody) + nonthermal components
- findings: Compares main burst vs postburst of GRB 130427A and GRB 160509A: both have thermal components, found mainly in the main bursts, while postbursts are dominated by nonthermal emission; some main-burst low-energy indices exceed the synchrotron death line, but none of 130427A's postburst bins do.

## Ruffini et al. (IGC analysis; arXiv 2014, published ~2015, journal not confirmed in search results)
- id: arXiv:1405.5723
- models: Time-resolved analysis in the induced-gravitational-collapse framework; power law + blackbody in soft X-rays
- findings: Multiwavelength analysis of GRB 130427A / SN 2013cq as an IGC event; evidences a blackbody component in the soft X-ray data at 196-461 s whose temperature decreases from 0.5 to 0.1 keV (adding BB efficiently improves the fit over a single power law in that interval).

## Ruffini et al. 2019, ApJ 886, 82
- id: arXiv:1812.00354
- models: BdHN 'inner engine' model (Kerr BH + uniform magnetic field); synchrotron emission for GeV/TeV
- findings: Uses GRB 130427A as the prototype type-I BdHN: proposes the GeV emission arises from an inner engine acting in a sequence of 'elementary impulses', with electrons in the magnetic field producing GeV-TeV synchrotron radiation. Interpretation-focused rather than empirical prompt model comparison.

## Ruffini et al. 2024 (A&A manuscript; 'Ten Supernova-rise in Binary Driven Gamma-ray Bursts')
- id: arXiv:2405.08231
- models: Band + blackbody for the SN-rise episode
- findings: Per the search snippet, the SN-rise episode of GRB 130427A lasted only 0.65 s (rest frame) and its spectrum is best fitted by Band + blackbody with a notably high blackbody temperature of 28.66 keV.

## Poolakkil et al. 2021, ApJ 913, 60 (Fermi GBM spectral catalog, 10 yr) — catalog entry
- id: arXiv:2103.13528 / 2021ApJ...913...60P
- models: Standard GBM catalog empirical models (four functions, e.g. Band/CPL/SBPL/PL), time-integrated + brightest-bin fits
- findings: Catalog-level spectral fits for 2297 GBM GRBs of the first 10 years (which includes bn130427324): time-integrated and peak-bin spectra with four empirical models; data products at HEASARC. Baseline catalog parameters for this burst, not a dedicated study.

## Tam et al. 2013, ApJL 771, L13
- id: arXiv:1305.3217 / 2013ApJ...771L..13T
- models: Time-resolved power-law fits of the LAT GeV emission; extra hard spectral component
- findings: Discovery of an extra hard spectral component in the high-energy AFTERGLOW emission of GRB 130427A, characterized from onset through the afterglow phase — evidence that a second, harder component coexists with the standard one at late times (afterglow, not prompt).

## Fan et al. 2013, ApJ 776, 95 (Fan, Tam, Zhang et al.)
- id: arXiv:1305.1261 / 2013ApJ...776...95F
- models: Forward-shock synchrotron + inverse-Compton modeling of the 100 MeV-100 GeV emission
- findings: Multi-instrument view (Swift, GBM/LAT, Konus-Wind, INTEGRAL SPI-ACS, AGILE, RHESSI); Eiso ~1e54 erg. The 100 MeV-100 GeV count rate is mainly forward-shock synchrotron, while inverse-Compton likely dominates at GeV-TeV; establishes an IC origin for the ~95, 47, 41, 38, 32 GeV photons at t ~ 243 s to 3.4e4 s (afterglow-phase, high-energy).

## Relevance to our program
GRB 130427A is one of the most heavily studied GBM bursts and touches nearly all of our questions: blackbody/photosphere (Preece's 4-sigma BB in the onset pulse; Ruffini-group Band+BB SN-rise with kT~28.7 keV; Du+2022 thermal-in-main-burst; late 0.5->0.1 keV BB), Ep evolution and synchrotron-vs-curvature in a single bright pulse (Preece), a second/extra hard component and high-energy behavior (Ackermann, Tam, Fan — though largely afterglow-phase), and low-energy indices vs the synchrotron death line (Du+2022). Crucially for our low-energy-break (2SBPL) question, Ravasio+2019 could NOT include this burst in their two-break sample because pulse pile-up deforms the main-pulse spectrum (only the precursor was analyzable) — any 2SBPL/Band+BB fits our pipeline makes on the main emission of bn130427324 carry a pile-up caveat, consistent with our existing Ronchi-cited low-E systematics note on the fig_spec_a example spectrum.
