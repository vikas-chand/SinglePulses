# bn210410037 — GRB 210410A (literature candidates)

## Li, Guo-Yu; Lin, Da-Bin; Li, Guo-Peng; Chen, Zhi-Lin; Zhang, Hai-Ming; Wang, Xiang-Gao; Liang, En-Wei 2023, ApJ 950, 78 — "Magnetar Giant Flare Origin for GRB 210410A?"
- id: 2023ApJ...950...78L (DOI 10.3847/1538-4357/acd003; no arXiv ID found — Semantic Scholar lists no preprint, arXiv API returns zero hits for this burst)
- models: Band function; CPL-mBB (cutoff power law + multicolor blackbody, i.e. non-dissipative photospheric emission model with q=3, s=1.2)
- findings: The burst is a very short-hard spike followed by extended tail emission (T90 ~ 48 s); spectra are very narrow with low-energy index alpha generally > -0.5, and are well fitted by non-dissipative photospheric emission (CPL-mBB), similar to GRB 200415A. In the Ep,z-Eiso/Ep,z-Liso planes it is consistent with either a short GRB at z~0.28 or a magnetar giant flare at ~2.7 Mpc, and it lies beyond the photospheric 'death line' of cosmic GRBs, suggesting a giant-flare-like origin; the paper also discusses the LAT extended emission including a 4.2 GeV photon ~30 s after trigger.

## Relevance to our program
Li et al. 2023 directly touches our blackbody/photosphere question: they find narrow spectra with hard alpha (>-0.5, above the synchrotron limit) fit by a non-dissipative photosphere (CPL-mBB), i.e. a thermal-dominated interpretation rather than a low-energy break/2SBPL — a useful cross-check for our BB-vs-two-break classification of this burst, and their high-E curvature (CPL-type) fit touches our cutoff question. Caveat for our single-pulse sample: this burst is a magnetar-giant-flare candidate (short-hard spike + tail), so its 'single-pulse' morphology may not be a classical cosmological GRB pulse; beyond this paper only GCN-level fits exist (GBM team time-averaged CPL; Konus-Wind detection) — the published GBM (Poolakkil 2021, 2008-2018) and LAT (2FLGC) catalog papers predate April 2021, so no published catalog-paper entry covers it.
