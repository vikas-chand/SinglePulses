# bn110721200 — GRB 110721A (literature candidates)

## Axelsson et al. 2012, ApJL 757, L31
- id: arXiv:1207.6109 / 2012ApJ...757L..31A
- models: Band; Band + blackbody (GBM + LAT LLE joint fits)
- findings: Time-resolved spectra are best modeled by a Band function plus a blackbody; the Band-component Ep is 15±2 MeV in the first time bin — the highest ever measured in a GRB — and decays as a power law with index -1.89±0.10. The blackbody temperature starts at ~80-100 keV and decreases with a significant break at ~2 s, interpreted as a photospheric signature.

## Zhang et al. 2012, ApJL 758, L34
- id: arXiv:1208.1812 / 2012ApJ...758L..34Z
- models: Band + blackbody decomposition; dissipative-photosphere theory comparison in the Ep-L plane
- findings: Defines a photosphere 'death line' in the Ep-L plane and shows the earliest-epoch Ep~15 MeV of GRB 110721A lies beyond it, so the Band component cannot be thermally dominated dissipative-photosphere emission and must be non-thermal (synchrotron or inverse Compton) from the optically thin region; the subdominant blackbody is the photosphere.

## Veres, Zhang & Meszaros 2012, ApJL 761, L18
- id: arXiv:1208.1790 / 2012ApJ...761L..18V
- models: Dissipative photosphere + synchrotron emission models (magnetically dominated to baryon dominated dynamics); internal-shock and standard blackbody photosphere models tested
- findings: The extreme ~15 MeV peak energy is unlikely in internal-shock models and a standard blackbody photospheric model also falls short, but dissipative photospheric synchrotron models spanning extreme magnetically dominated to baryon-dominated dynamics can accommodate such high Ep values.

## Iyyani et al. 2013, MNRAS 433, 2739
- id: arXiv:1305.3611 / 2013MNRAS.433.2739I
- models: Band + blackbody (time-resolved); photospheric outflow-parameter inference
- findings: Time-resolved spectra are best modeled with a blackbody (kT ~100 keV, decaying with a break at ~2 s) plus a Band function (Ep ~1 MeV); the blackbody is used to track the jet photosphere, yielding time-variable jet/outflow properties (Lorentz factor, photospheric radius) during the burst.

## Burgess et al. 2014, ApJ 784, 17
- id: arXiv:1304.4628 / 2014ApJ...784...17B
- models: Physical fast- and slow-cooled synchrotron photon models + blackbody; compared against Band and other non-thermal empirical models
- findings: In a time-resolved study of 8 bright GBM bursts including GRB 110721A, slow-cooled synchrotron plus a blackbody fits the data well (blackbody required in 5/8 bursts); fit statistics for fast-cooled synchrotron vs other non-thermal models are compared explicitly for GRB 110721A, and the empirical alpha ~ -0.81±0.1 sits between fast- and slow-cooling limits.

## Gao & Zhang 2015, ApJ 801, 103
- id: arXiv:1409.3584 / 2015ApJ...801..103G
- models: Hybrid relativistic outflow photosphere model (arbitrary dimensionless entropy eta and magnetization sigma_0); top-down diagnosis of the Band+BB decomposition
- findings: Applies a 'top-down' method to GRB 110721A's published blackbody-component evolution and shows it is well interpreted by a time-varying (eta, sigma_0) hybrid (fireball + Poynting flux) central engine, as an alternative to a changing engine base size.

## Yu et al. 2016, A&A 588, A135 (GBM time-resolved spectral catalog, brightest bursts)
- id: arXiv:1601.05206 / 2016A&A...588A.135Y
- models: Catalog empirical models per time bin: Band, Comptonized/CPL, smoothly broken power law, power law
- findings: Catalog entry (81 brightest GBM bursts, 1491 spectra): GRB 110721A (trigger 110721200) holds the largest peak energy in the catalog, Ep = 7409±597 keV; catalog-wide, most time-resolved spectra are best fit by the exponential cutoff power-law (high-energy tail sharper than Band).

## Fraija et al. 2017, ApJ 848, 94
- id: arXiv:1709.06263 / 2017ApJ...848...94F
- models: External-shock afterglow modeling: forward-shock synchrotron + reverse-shock synchrotron self-Compton in a stellar-wind medium (not prompt spectral decomposition)
- findings: Models the Fermi-LAT high-energy emission of GRB 110721A as early external-shock forward-shock synchrotron plus reverse-shock SSC in a wind environment, and discusses implications for the early multiwavelength and IKAROS/GAP polarimetric observations; the prompt keV-MeV Band+BB decomposition is taken from prior work rather than re-fit.

## Li 2019, ApJS 245, 7 ('Thermal Components in Gamma-Ray Bursts. I.')
- id: arXiv:1905.02340 / doi:10.3847/1538-4365/ab42de
- models: Band, Band+BB, CPL+BB comparisons across 8 GBM bursts with significant thermal components
- findings: Places GRB 110721A in the thermal-subdominant group (Group I, vs thermal-dominant bursts like 090902B) and quantifies how ignoring the blackbody biases the non-thermal parameters: the low-energy photon index hardens and Ep shifts when the BB is omitted, with the bias correlated with the thermal-to-total flux ratio.

## Relevance to our program
GRB 110721A is the canonical Band+BB photosphere burst, so the literature directly anchors two of our questions: the blackbody/Ep-kT question (Axelsson+12, Iyyani+13, Li19 all require a subdominant BB with kT ~30-100 keV declining with a ~2 s break, and Li19 quantifies exactly the Band-parameter bias our BB-vs-no-BB comparison probes) and the peak/extreme-Ep question (record Ep ~15 MeV early, catalog Ep 7409 keV in Yu+16; Zhang+12 and Veres+12 debate synchrotron vs dissipative-photosphere origin). Crucially, NO published work fits this burst with a 2SBPL/low-energy-break model — it is absent from the Ravasio+19 two-break sample — so our pipeline's two-break (2SBPL) result for bn110721200 would be a new claim that must be reconciled with the established Band+BB degeneracy (a BB on the low-energy shoulder can mimic a low-energy break).
