# bn081224887 — GRB 081224 (long, bright single-pulse FRED, 2008-12-24 21:17:55 UT; GBM-style name GRB 081224887; note a second same-day trigger bn081224060 exists, distinct from this burst). Name resolved via GCN 8723 "GRB 081224: Fermi GBM Detection & LAT Localization" (Wilson-Hodge et al. 2008). (literature candidates)

## Lu, Wei, Liang, Zhang et al. 2012, ApJ 756, 112 (A Comprehensive Analysis of Fermi GRB Data: II. Ep-Evolution Patterns)
- id: arXiv:1204.0714 (2012ApJ...756..112L)
- models: Band (time-resolved, RMFIT); Ep-evolution classification
- findings: GRB 081224 is one of 5 single-pulse long GRBs (of 8) with clean hard-to-soft Ep evolution (alongside 090809B, 100612A, 100707A, 110817A); its per-bin Band fits are tabulated. It is also one of two templates for their pulse-superposition simulation showing overlapping HTS pulses can mimic intensity-tracking, with mock superposed spectra still well fit by the Band function.

## Tashiro, Onda, Yamaoka, Ohno, Sugita, Uehara & Seta 2012, PASJ 64, 26 (Spectral Evolutions in GRB Exponential Decays Observed with Suzaku WAM)
- id: arXiv:1110.4246
- models: power law, cutoff power law, blackbody + power law (BB+PL), Band; energy-resolved decay time constants tau(E) ~ E^gamma
- findings: For GRB 081224 the 1-s time-resolved decay-phase WAM spectra are well described by a blackbody + power-law model, i.e. a cooling blackbody, with cooling consistent with radiative cooling or expansion of the emission region (unlike other sample bursts better matched by thin-plasma emission). Average decay-time energy index gamma ~ -0.34 +/- 0.12 across the 6-burst FRED sample.

## Ackermann et al. 2013, ApJS 209, 11 (The First Fermi-LAT Gamma-Ray Burst Catalog)
- id: arXiv:1303.2908 (2013ApJS..209...11A)
- models: LAT likelihood (>100 MeV) + LLE counting analysis; no burst-specific spectral fit for 081224
- findings: GRB 081224 is explicitly discussed but NOT included in the final catalog table: despite a tentative onboard LAT localization circulated via GCN (Wilson-Hodge et al. 2008), it was formally below the catalog detection threshold on ground analysis. Caveat: later claims of 'LAT detection' for this burst (e.g. Basak & Rao 2014) trace back to this marginal/onboard signal — verify against the actual papers.

## Basak & Rao 2014, MNRAS 442, 419 (Time-resolved spectral study of Fermi GRBs having single pulses)
- id: arXiv:1405.0119
- models: Band, BBPL (blackbody+power law), mBBPL (multicolour BB+PL), 2BBPL (two blackbodies+PL)
- findings: In their single-pulse GBM sample, models beyond BBPL (mBBPL/2BBPL) give superior fits for hard-to-soft pulses, and low-energy photon indices often exceed the single-particle synchrotron limit. GRB 081224 is singled out as one of three HTS bursts (with reported LAT detections) in which the power-law component shows a delayed onset and lingers after the pulse.

## Yu, Dereli-Bégué & Ryde 2019, ApJ 886, 20 (Bayesian Time-Resolved Spectroscopy of GRB Pulses)
- id: arXiv:1810.07313
- models: cutoff power law (CPL) and Band, fully Bayesian time-resolved fits
- findings: GRB 081224 is in their sample of 38 single pulses from 37 GBM bursts (first 9 yr), explicitly classified as a single-pulse GRB; 577 spectra total are fit with the two empirical models and their parameter distributions/evolutions studied. Burst-specific parameter values for 081224 need to be read from their tables (not in the snippets).

## Meng, Liu, Wei, Wu & Zhang 2019, ApJ 882, 26 (The Time-resolved Spectra of Photospheric Emission from a Structured Jet for GRBs)
- id: arXiv:1904.08526 (10.3847/1538-4357/ab30c7)
- models: structured-jet photospheric emission model (inner-constant, outer-decreasing Lorentz-factor profile) compared against CPL-like time-resolved fits
- findings: Uses GRB 081224 as an observed single-pulse comparison case (their Fig. 13 shows its time-resolved best-fit spectra, e.g. intervals 2.370-3.037 s and 8.019-13.930 s): rising-phase spectra are CPL-like with alpha ~ -1, and the observed spectral evolution is similar to their structured-jet photosphere prediction, comparable to GRB 090809B and GRB 110817A. Note their abstract does not name the burst — the comparison is in the body; verify against the full paper.

## Wang, Xie, Gao, Xiao, Dong, Zhang & Zhi 2024, Research in Astronomy and Astrophysics 24, 025006 (Time-resolved Spectral Properties of Fermi-GBM Bright Long GRBs)
- id: arXiv:2401.14969
- models: Band (time-resolved fits of 1490 spectra from 78 bright long GBM GRBs)
- findings: bn081224887 is in their sample (detectors n6, n7, b1; 16 time bins) and its spectral evolution is classified hard-to-soft. Sample-wide they find typical alpha ~ -0.72, beta ~ -2.42, Ep ~ 222 keV, >80% of bursts exceed the synchrotron line of death in their hardest bins, single-pulse GRBs preferentially show HTS evolution, and flux correlates positively with Ep — interpreted as photospheric + synchrotron mechanisms coexisting.

## Bordoloi, Shivkumar & Iyyani 2026 (Ep-alpha Correlation in Time Resolved GRB Spectra: A Bottom-Up Approach with Optically Thin Inverse Compton Scattering Model)
- id: arXiv:2606.04894
- models: optically thin inverse-Compton-scattering (ICS) emission model vs time-resolved empirical (Ep, alpha) fits; comparison to hybrid two-composition jet models
- findings: Cites GRB 081224 (with GRB 090719A and GRB 100707A) as a burst where alpha evolves hard-to-soft together with Ep within the same emission episode, with alpha initially positive or well above the synchrotron line of death (alpha > -0.67) before dropping below it later; used to exemplify the evolution pattern their ICS model explains, not analyzed individually in depth.

## Relevance to our program
GRB 081224 is a canonical bright single-pulse FRED with a rich spectral literature that touches nearly all of our questions: (1) blackbody/photosphere — Tashiro+2012 (cooling BB+PL in the decay), Basak & Rao 2014 (BBPL/mBBPL/2BBPL preferred for HTS pulses), and Meng+2019 (structured-jet photosphere reproduces its evolution) make it a prime Ep–kT / thermal-component test case; (2) low-energy index/break — repeated findings of alpha above the synchrotron line of death early in the pulse (Basak & Rao, Wang+2024, Bordoloi+2026) bear directly on our low-energy-break vs blackbody (2SBPL vs +BB) curvature question; (3) spectral evolution — unanimous hard-to-soft classification (Lu+2012, Wang+2024) feeds our rise-phase HTS/IT census, and Lu+2012 even uses this burst as the template for the HTS-superposition-mimics-tracking argument; (4) second component/high energy — a delayed-onset, lingering power-law component with a marginal (below first-LAT-catalog threshold) LAT/LLE signal is relevant to our second-component question. No dedicated single-burst paper was found; coverage is via samples and catalogs. All entries are candidates pending verification against the full papers.
